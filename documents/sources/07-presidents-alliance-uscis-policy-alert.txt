Explainer: USCIS Policy Alert on Deferred Action and Possible Implications for DACA

This resource is provided for informational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Individuals should consult with qualified counsel regarding their specific circumstances.

On May 8, 2026, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) issued a new Policy Alert titled "Deferred Action as an Extraordinary Use of Prosecutorial Discretion." The alert indicates that USCIS has released updated policy guidance in the USCIS Policy Manual emphasizing that deferred action functions as a restricted and extraordinary exercise of prosecutorial discretion intended only for compelling individual circumstances.

While the policy alert addresses deferred action broadly, this resource emphasizes potential implications for Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA), particularly regarding how USCIS may apply discretion when handling pending and future DACA renewals.

Key Takeaways:

- The guidance does not eliminate DACA, modify existing eligibility requirements, or revoke current DACA approvals. However, it suggests a potentially more restrictive approach to USCIS discretion in adjudicating pending and future DACA applications.

- Even when individuals satisfy baseline DACA criteria, USCIS maintains discretion to evaluate newly established criteria before granting deferred action.

- For pending DACA renewals: This guidance became effective immediately and applies to applications pending or filed on or after May 8, 2026, including renewals already submitted but not yet adjudicated. Consulting an attorney is recommended for upcoming or pending renewals.

- USCIS describes deferred action as an "extraordinary" application of prosecutorial discretion.

- The policy alert details discretionary factors USCIS considers when determining whether deferred action warrants approval.

- The agency opposes expansive categorical grants of deferred action unless specifically permitted by law or regulation.

- USCIS contends that individual reliance interests in obtaining or preserving deferred action do not supersede governmental interests in maintaining immigration system integrity, national security, and public safety.

- Individuals with potentially unfavorable factors under the new guidance may benefit from working with legal counsel when submitting renewal applications.

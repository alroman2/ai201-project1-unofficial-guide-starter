DACA Renewal in 2026

Overview

Current DACA recipients can renew their status nationwide as of March 2026. The renewal fee is $555 for online filing or $605 for mail submission. USCIS recommends filing 120-150 days before expiration.

Key Facts

Renewal Eligibility: Yes, current DACA holders can renew despite court rulings declaring the program unlawful. A stay allows renewals to continue while litigation proceeds.

Fees: No fee waivers are available, though some nonprofits offer financial assistance or zero-interest loans to cover costs.

Processing: USCIS historical data shows the median DACA renewal processing time was about one month in fiscal year 2023, though recent reports indicate potential delays.

Required Forms:
- Form I-821D (deferred action request)
- Form I-765 (employment authorization)
- Form I-765WS (economic worksheet)

Legal Status

The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled in January 2025 that deportation protection is lawful, but work authorization may be limited to non-Texas areas. Judge Andrew Hanen in Texas could issue a modified order separating these components.

Important Concerns

ICE arrested 261 DACA recipients between January-November 2025. DACA termination can occur for criminal convictions or national security concerns.

Florida Impact

Approximately 20,000-21,000 DACA recipients live in Florida (4% of national total).

Alternative Options

Recipients should explore family-based green cards, VAWA claims, U visas, asylum, or employer sponsorship as permanent alternatives.
